# Méthode Secondaire Managed Tutoring Agency — Operating Design

**Date:** 2026-07-26  
**Decision owner:** Chahine / Méthode Secondaire  
**Planning horizon:** 2026-07-27 to 2026-10-24

## Decision

Méthode Secondaire will develop as a managed tutoring service, not a marketplace of independent tutors. The company will remain visibly specialized in Québec secondary-school support while it builds a controlled extension into French, English, and selected CEGEP subjects.

The immediate strategy is to create trained capacity and a repeatable service before the school-year demand surge. No custom operations software, new student AI product, unlimited messaging offer, or broad all-subject marketing will be built in this 90-day period. The existing website, CRM, session-notes workflow, payment process, and parent portal are sufficient for the first operating version.

## Outcome by 24 October 2026

Méthode Secondaire can confidently support an initial cohort of 20 active students with a service that is consistent even when Chahine is not the tutor.

The operating target is:

- Three active non-owner tutors in total, including the existing tutor and two new hires.
- One additional trained backup tutor.
- At least 24 to 28 realistic recurring weekly session slots across the team, including the owner, with evening and weekend coverage explicitly recorded.
- Every active student has an assigned tutor, current priority, next evaluation or milestone where known, and a next action in the CRM.
- Every completed session has a tutor note within 12 hours; every active family receives a concise structured update at least once every four weeks.
- A tutor absence, poor fit, or high-risk student has an owner and a documented response path.

The capacity target deliberately includes spare slots. Twenty weekly students should not consume every available hour; response speed and continuity are part of the premium product.

## Positioning and offer boundaries

### Core public promise — launch now

> Méthode Secondaire provides managed academic support for Québec secondary students. Families are matched with trained tutors, receive clear follow-up, and can rely on the company for continuity and quality.

The core service lines are:

- Secondary 1–5 mathematics.
- Secondary 1–5 science, including biology-related support where it matches the Québec curriculum.
- Secondary 1–5 French.
- Secondary 1–5 English.

The current session and block catalogue remains the starting commercial structure. It should be described as a guided program of support, not merely purchased tutor hours. Pricing is not changed in this plan; the company will first record actual conversion, tutor cost, administrative time, and retention data before changing prices or discounts.

### Controlled CEGEP pilot — train now, sell selectively later

CEGEP support is a separate pilot, not the main brand promise during this 90-day period. Initial eligible subjects are biology and philosophy, plus another CEGEP subject only where an approved tutor is demonstrably qualified.

The website may say that *selected CEGEP support is available*, but it must not promise broad CEGEP coverage. A CEGEP subject can be publicly promoted only after all four launch gates are met:

1. A trained, approved tutor has at least five realistic weekly slots in that subject.
2. The subject has a course-level first-session diagnostic and a short explanation of the service scope.
3. The tutor has completed a mock session and submitted an acceptable sample parent update.
4. A backup, replacement, or transparent wait-list path exists.

This preserves the strength of the Méthode Secondaire name while allowing deliberate expansion. If CEGEP grows into a material share of demand, it will receive a distinct sub-brand or navigation section later; that decision is outside this 90-day plan.

## Service system

### Parent relationship

Méthode Secondaire owns onboarding, matching, payments, scheduling policy, satisfaction follow-up, parent reporting, renewals, and tutor replacements. Tutors own instruction and the warm day-to-day teaching relationship.

Parents should always know that a problem has a company-level owner. Tutors must not independently promise an ongoing schedule, change a price, or resolve a serious fit issue without the company workflow.

### Standard student journey

1. The parent submits a short request or speaks with the company.
2. Méthode Secondaire qualifies the need, checks capacity, and matches a tutor before confirming a time or requesting payment.
3. The first session establishes the student’s priority, prerequisite gaps, learning habits, and next milestone.
4. Each later session follows a shared structure: check-in and prior work; teach or correct the priority; guided practice; independent attempt; next-step recommendation.
5. The tutor submits a concise session note within 12 hours.
6. The company sends a useful update after the first session and a structured progress update every four weeks while the student is active.
7. A `watch` or `high` risk note creates a named follow-up action; poor fit triggers a replacement or owner-led intervention.

### Minimum tutor quality system

The future teaching playbook is deliberately short enough to be used. It will define:

- First-session diagnostic questions and evidence to collect.
- The standard 60-minute session structure.
- Required tutor-note fields and the definition of a useful parent summary.
- Escalation triggers: persistent prerequisite gap, no progress, attendance issue, parent concern, poor tutor fit, or an imminent exam.
- Communication boundaries and scheduling/payment authority.
- A quality check: mock session before the first client, review after the first client, and a quarterly observation or note audit.

Subject expertise remains essential. The common system makes the client experience dependable without making tutors sound identical.

## Summer staffing plan

### Required roles

Recruit against verified demand gaps, not generic availability:

1. **Secondary math/science tutor:** priority hire if the existing tutor does not already cover the evenings and levels most requested.
2. **Secondary French/English tutor:** priority hire to support the expansion while keeping the core audience.
3. **Backup or CEGEP-capable tutor:** a candidate qualified for CEGEP biology, philosophy, or another proven need; activated only after training and capacity confirmation.

One candidate may cover more than one role only if their subject competence, availability, and teaching demonstration all meet the standard. A high-quality single-subject tutor is preferable to a broadly advertised but weak generalist.

### Candidate approval gates

No candidate receives a student solely because they are available. The approval sequence is:

1. Application and résumé screened against subject, level, language, format, and evening/weekend availability.
2. Short interview assessing explanation skill, reliability, and attitude toward parent communication.
3. A 20–30 minute observed mock lesson using a realistic Québec secondary or CEGEP problem.
4. Reference and identity checks appropriate to working with minors, completed according to the company’s legal and safeguarding obligations.
5. Onboarding: CRM, availability, session notes, parent relationship boundaries, and payment/scheduling rules.
6. First student assigned on probation; the first session note and parent experience are reviewed by Chahine.

## Operations and source of truth

The existing Google Sheets CRM remains the operational source of truth for identifiable client data. It already has leads, tutor roster, availability, sessions, notes, payments, enrollments, and credit records.

The two new repository documents will be management tools only. They will contain targets, checklists, decisions, and aggregate counts; they will not copy student or parent personal information.

- **90-day execution plan:** the full sequence of work, definition of done, and verification for each deliverable.
- **Weekly operating tracker:** short recurring review of capacity, recruiting, service quality, parent updates, risks, and decisions.

The tracker will be updated weekly during the summer, then twice weekly from the first two weeks of the school year until matching demand stabilizes.

## Metrics and decision rules

The company will track a small set of indicators, not a decorative dashboard:

| Area | Measure | Initial standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity | Confirmed recurring weekly slots / expected weekly students | At least 1.2× before accepting ongoing matches |
| Matching | Parent request to confirmed response | Same business day for urgent, otherwise within 24 business hours |
| Tutor operations | Completed sessions with a note submitted within 12 hours | 95% or higher |
| Parent experience | Active students with an update in the past 28 days | 100% |
| Continuity | Active students with a named replacement or wait-list response path | 100% |
| Quality | New tutors with a reviewed mock lesson and reviewed first-client note | 100% |
| Commercial health | Revenue, tutor pay, processing cost, and admin time by offer | Measured before pricing changes |

If capacity falls below 1.2× anticipated ongoing demand, marketing expansion pauses and hiring/matching becomes the priority. If a new subject does not meet its launch gates, it remains off-menu rather than being sold with an uncertain tutor match.

## 90-day sequencing

### Phase 1 — Establish the operating truth (27 July–2 August)

Confirm actual active students, expected fall requests, tutor capacity by subject/level/time, existing tutor availability, and current offer economics. Finalize recruiting scorecard, interview questions, mock-lesson rubric, job post, and candidate pipeline.

**Exit condition:** a visible capacity gap by subject and time, active candidate sourcing, and a weekly review cadence.

### Phase 2 — Recruit and qualify capacity (3–23 August)

Source candidates, screen them, run interviews and mock lessons, complete appropriate checks, and record every approved tutor’s availability. The goal is two new trained active tutors plus a backup candidate; the CEGEP-capable role may remain backup until its subject gate is satisfied.

**Exit condition:** the team has realistic recorded availability sufficient for the anticipated initial cohort, not merely verbal interest.

### Phase 3 — Standardize and rehearse the service (17–30 August)

Create the short teaching playbook, first-session diagnostic, subject-launch checklist, escalation path, and four-week parent progress update. Each approved tutor completes a mock session and submits a sample note and parent update.

**Exit condition:** every active tutor can follow the process without Chahine improvising each step.

### Phase 4 — Controlled school-year launch (31 August–27 September)

Match students through the CRM, protect the response SLA, review all first-session notes, send the first parent updates, and correct failure points quickly. Assign no tutor above confirmed capacity. Pilot a CEGEP inquiry only if the subject’s four launch gates are met.

**Exit condition:** the system has served real students for four weeks with reliable notes, updates, and replacements.

### Phase 5 — Improve from evidence (28 September–24 October)

Review conversion by subject, capacity utilization, parent feedback, session-note quality, risks, retention, and unit economics. Strengthen the weakest operating step before investing in new marketing, automation, or AI features.

**Exit condition:** an evidence-based decision on adding each CEGEP pilot subject, changing capacity targets, and selecting the next website or operations improvement.

## Explicit non-goals for this period

- Building a custom CRM, mobile app, or a large course library.
- Advertising every subject, grade, or CEGEP course.
- Selling unlimited between-session support.
- Allowing tutors to independently own billing, scheduling policy, or company-level client escalation.
- Raising or redesigning all prices before operational and unit-economics data is collected.
- Presenting AI as a substitute for human diagnosis, accountability, or curriculum-aligned teaching.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| A low-quality tutor damages trust | Mock lesson, probation, first-note review, and fast replacement process |
| Tutors have availability but not peak-time availability | Record recurring evening/weekend slots before any promise to a parent |
| Subject expansion makes the company look generic | Keep secondary specialization as the main message; gate individual CEGEP subjects |
| Parent reporting creates too much admin | One short structured update every four weeks; tutors supply the factual note |
| Chahine remains the bottleneck | Use the CRM, playbook, quality checks, and weekly tracker to transfer routine operations without transferring responsibility |
| Demand exceeds trained capacity | Wait-list honestly, use the backup tutor, and pause marketing expansion rather than lowering standards |

## Design self-review

- No unspecified software build or hidden subscription is required.
- The public promise, subject scope, staffing target, tutor approval rules, and measurement rules are consistent.
- CEGEP is explicitly a controlled pilot, avoiding a conflict with the secondary-school brand.
- Personal student data remains in the existing CRM, not in repository planning documents.
- The strategy is sized for the next 90 days and can be implemented incrementally with small, independently useful deliverables.
